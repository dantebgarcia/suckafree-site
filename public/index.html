Mission

Why we hop on the back of the bus

What we're here to do

Sucka Free Collective supports working artists rooted in the San Francisco Bay Area by cultivating accessible spaces where artists, neighbors and community members can make, learn, rehearse, celebrate, and care for one another.

Through shared creative resources, affordable workspace, cultural programming and skill sharing, we are building a home where people move from attending events to becoming collaborators, contributors and community stewards.

We are based in the Bayview, one of San Francisco's last Black neighborhoods, and we are committed to celebrating and growing San Francisco born and raised artists.

The warehouse was the home of Hanson Lee's electrical company. Meymey's father kept it going until the 2008 financial crisis made the space hard to sustain. After the first Muni Raised Me show in 2023, the bus needed a home, and in 2025 it moved in. At the same time, artists and art spaces across the city were being displaced. So we formed a collective to run the warehouse and the bus as a shared space, and built it out by hand, mostly with donated materials and finds from SCRAP.

We have been a collective since Muni Raised Me in 2023. We do this for the city kids, as city kids. As Maya Angelou put it: when you get, give; when you learn, teach.

Culture pre-dates politics.

Shared values

“Abolition is about presence, not absence. It’s about building life-affirming institutions.” Ruth Wilson Gilmore

Accessibility

Sliding scale dues, an ADA accessible first floor, free parking and a train nearby. Getting in the door should never be the hard part.

Radical imagination

We live, create and dream with the most radical of imaginations so that our most radical dreams become reality.

Joy

Clothing swaps, dance parties, craft nights and live shows. Joyful creative spaces make stronger neighborhoods and better organizing.

Muni Raised Me

Sucka Free grew out of Muni Raised Me, a queer, BIPOC-led community arts project celebrating the role San Francisco's public transit system has played in connecting and raising generations of working class city residents.

The first show opened at SOMArts in the spring of 2023: a gathering of the hyphy and the rooted, featuring all San Francisco born and raised artists. More than 800 people came through for the opening and closing.

Its centerpiece, Altared SF, is a retired Muni bus that SFMTA gifted a month before the opening of the SOMArts exhibition, transformed into a mobile gallery, living altar and immersive installation. Today the bus lives at our warehouse and still rolls out to community activations across the city. Muni Raised Me 2 opens here on April 15, 2027.

An arts resilience hub

What we're building toward

We are growing a network around a place: the warehouse and the Bayview's community, ecology and infrastructure. The goal is a space where artists get ready for hard times together and show up for the neighborhood when they come.

Practice

A demonstration space where artists practice preparedness, with readiness trainings alongside the art.

Prepare

A place to build and store what we will need: shared storage, a loading door, a big ADA accessible ground floor, free parking and the T line nearby.

Show up

A staging point for mutual aid. In an emergency the bus can carry supplies, serve as a mobile check-in point and bring readiness training to other arts spaces.

The network starts with core members who have studios, storage or rehearsal time here, then the regulars who return to our gatherings, then other artist-run spaces in southeast San Francisco. Bayview–Hunters Point (94124) first. Meymey Lee and Dante Garcia co-lead with a steering circle of core members. We are planning with Kayaks Unlimited, who steward Islais Creek Park across the street, and With the Wild Things, who train in first aid and rescue. Shared gear, skills and contacts are tracked on Community Gearbox.

The Space

4,000 square feet in the Bayview

A former electrical warehouse, rewired

Two stories, two garage doors and room for a retired Muni bus. The warehouse is family owned and commercially zoned, with studios to make work in and an open floor to share it on.

Upstairs there is shared studio space for 8 artists and 3 private studios, rented month to month at prices working artists can afford. Most are taken right now; ask about openings. Downstairs, the stage was built from scrap wood, the sound and lights started out borrowed, and much of the room came from donations and SCRAP. Young artists with large-scale projects store work here when they have nowhere else to put it.

Coming next: a recording studio, a washout booth to finish the screen printing studio (and open print days after that), and a shared gear library with training so members can use the tools on their own.

Book the warehouse for an event, a workshop or training, a rehearsal, a retreat or a meeting. Rentals help pay for the free and low cost programming we run for the neighborhood.

Free for community, twice a month

We open the space free of charge to community groups at least twice a month, case by case. Tell us what you are planning in the form and mention it.

How booking works

  • Tell us about your event: what it is, when, how many people, your budget, what you need, and how it fits what we are about.
  • We check the calendar and get back to you.
  • Everyone signs our event agreement, so we all know what is provided and what guests are agreeing to.
  • A collective member is on site. Bigger events bring security and a cleanup plan.

You can also book Altared SF, the bus, as part of your event. Past events here: concerts, raves, DJ nights, listening parties, plays, birthday parties, funerals, Indigenous dance ceremonies, workshops and organizing meetings.
